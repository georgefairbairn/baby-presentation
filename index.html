Forty Weeks, Three Trimesters

Each trimester has its own character. The first is hard and hidden, the second is the good one, the third is a waiting game.

First · Weeks 1–12

  • Every organ formsWhich is why this trimester matters most, and why it's the riskiest.
  • Dating scan at 8–10 weeksA blob with a heartbeat.
  • Morning sicknessAll day (not just in the morning). Usually eases by week 14.
  • Real exhaustionBlood volume climbs 50% while the body builds a placenta from nothing.

Second · Weeks 13–26

  • The golden trimesterNausea backs off, energy comes back. Most people feel best here.
  • First kicks, 18–22 weeksFlutters at first. Easy to mistake for indigestion.
  • Anatomy scan at 20 weeksWhen we could find out the sex - but we're not gonna!
  • Stretching achesRound ligament pain, back pain, and the bump arrives.

Third · Weeks 27–40

  • Baby grows at half a pound a week2lb at week 27, 6–9lb by birth.
  • Braxton HicksPractice contractions. Uncomfortable, not painful. Regular and painful is different.
  • Strep B swab at 35–37 weeksA quarter of women carry it. If positive, antibiotics in labour.
  • Sleep becomes impossibleNothing is comfortable.

What to Eat, What to Skip

The rules sound overwhelming written down. The short version is nothing raw, nothing mould-ripened, and more iron than feels necessary.

Plenty of

  • IronRed meat, spinach, lentils. Blood volume increases substantially, and low iron means even more exhaustion. Pair with vitamin C.
  • Oily fish, twice a weekBrain development. Capped at twice because of mercury.
  • Calcium and whole grainsDairy or fortified plant milk, leafy greens, legumes.
  • Folic acid, vitamin D, a prenatalFolic acid is the important one. It prevents neural tube defects (we started in April).

None of

  • AlcoholThere's no known safe amount, so the answer is none.
  • Raw or undercooked anythingSushi, runny eggs, rare meat. Listeria and toxoplasmosis are rare but serious.
  • Soft cheese, pâté, deli meatsBrie, camembert, and sadly prosciutto on a pizza.
  • High-mercury fishSwordfish and shark specifically.
Caffeine doesn't have to be zero. Health Canada says under 300mg a day, which is about two coffees.

Before We Start

You

  • Folic acid, 400mcg dailyIdeally three months before we start.
  • Medication reviewSome prescriptions aren't safe in pregnancy.
  • Rubella and chickenpox immunityBoth are live vaccines, so they have to happen before conception with a month's gap after.
  • Cycle trackingSo we know the fertile window rather than guessing.

Me

  • Sperm takes ~3 months to turn overSo anything I change now shows up later, not immediately.
  • Less alcohol, more sleepUnglamorous, but it's the whole list.
  • Better diet

If it takes a while

  • Roughly 1 in 4 per cycleAbout 85% of couples conceive within a year. A few months is the normal case, not the bad case.
  • Ask for help at 12 monthsFirst tests are simple, and we both get tested.
  • BC funds one IVF cycleUp to $19,000, but it stops at $250k household. So we'd get no help here!

When to Call

🔴 Call immediately

  • Heavy bleedingEspecially with cramping.
  • Severe abdominal pain
  • Baby stops movingThird trimester.
  • Preeclampsia signsSudden swelling, severe headache, vision changes.
  • High fever

🟡 Within 24 hours

  • Reduced movementReduced, not absent.
  • Painful urinationOften a UTI. Common in pregnancy.
  • Persistent vomiting
  • Unusual discharge

🟢 Next appointment

  • Mild spottingFirst trimester.
  • Braxton HicksPractice contractions.
  • General aches and swellingHeartburn too.
When in doubt, red. Nobody has ever been annoyed at a pregnant woman for calling. HealthLink BC is 811 if we just want to ask someone.

What Can Go Wrong

Miscarriage

  • 1 in 8 known pregnanciesAround 80% of those happen in the first 12 weeks.
  • Chromosomal, about half the timeIt happens at conception. Not caused by anything either of us did.

Ectopic

  • 1 in 90 pregnanciesSharp one-sided pain, shoulder-tip pain, dizziness.
  • This one is a straight to A&E job

Preeclampsia

  • After week 20Sudden swelling, severe headaches, vision changes.
  • More common in first pregnanciesPicked up at routine BP checks.

Gestational diabetes

  • Mid-second trimesterUsually no symptoms at all.
  • Routine screening catches itAnd it's manageable.
The reassuring half. Risk drops fast. 1.7% at 12 weeks, 0.5% by 16, and it falls significantly once there's a heartbeat. Around 85% of pregnancies go on healthily. Even after two or three losses, 75% of couples still get there. We will focus on what we can control and know that the rest is out of our control.

What goes on it

  • Who's in the roomUs, family, or nobody.
  • Pain reliefWhat you'd like to try first, and how you feel about intervention.
  • Skin-to-skin and cord clampingDelayed clamping if all is well.

How to treat it

  • Draft it end of the second trimesterDiscuss it with our provider around week 36.
  • One page, maximumNobody reads two pages during a labour.

Part TwoBirth & Hospital

How long is the average first-time labour?

A) 4–6 hours
B) 8–10 hours
C) 12–18 hours
D) 24+ hours
Only 4–8 of those are activeThe rest is early labour, and most of it is best spent at home.
Second babies are much fasterOften half the time. Nobody's first is the benchmark.
It's an average, not a targetShorter and longer are both completely normal.

How Labour Actually Goes

Four stages. The first is by far the longest, and most of it happens before we go anywhere.

1a · Early labour

  • 0 to 6cmThe longest stage by a distance.
  • Contractions start irregularSlowly building to a rhythm.
  • Best spent at homeEat, bath, rest, distract. Sleep if it starts at night.

1b · Active labour

  • 6 to 10cmThis is the serious part.
  • Stronger, longer, closer togetherThe pattern gets more consistent.
  • Pain relief decisions land hereUsually not before.

2 · Pushing

  • 20 minutes to 3 hoursFor a first baby. Huge range.
  • CrowningThe "ring of fire". Intense stretch, then numbness.
  • Head comes out (unless you're Angus – arse first), then shoulder, then the restAnd then baby is plopped onto your chest.

3 · The placenta

  • Nobody warns you about this oneThere is a second delivery.
  • 5 to 30 minutesWith an injection. Up to an hour without.
  • Much easierThan what just happened.
5-1-1 is when we go to the hospital. Contractions five minutes apart, lasting one minute, for one hour. Also go for waters breaking, heavy bleeding, or reduced movement. And go anyway if your instinct says go.

Pain Relief Options in Canada

All available, all covered. Worth having a first preference so nobody has to ask you a question mid-contraction.

EpiduralThe most effective by far. Needs an anaesthetist, available at most hospitals.
Nitrous oxideGas and air. Widely available, take it or leave it between contractions.
IV opioidsMorphine or fentanyl. Takes the edge off rather than removing it.
WaterBath or shower. Genuinely good in early labour.
TENS machineBest used early. Needs hiring in advance, so it's a thing to remember.
Nothing at allBreathing, movement, massage, hypnobirthing. A real option, not a lesser one.
You can change your mind at any point. The only catch is that not every option is available at every stage, so the earlier we make a decision the more choices we have.

The First Few Hours

The golden hour

  • Skin-to-skin, straight awayRegulates their temperature, heart rate and breathing. Starts the bonding.
  • Delayed cord clampingWaiting 1–3 minutes measurably boosts their iron stores.
  • First feed within the hourIf it works. If it doesn't, that's also fine.
  • If you can't do the skin to skin for whatever reason, I do itWith my juicy melons they won't know the difference!

You

  • Stitches if neededUnder local anaesthetic.
  • Vitals watched for a couple of hoursStandard, not a warning sign.
  • Take the pain reliefIbuprofen and paracetamol together work properly.
  • You might be shakingThat's adrenaline, and it's normal.
  • Bleeding lasts 4–6 weeksMaternity pads, not tampons.

Baby

  • Apgar at 1 and 5 minutesHeart rate, breathing, colour, tone, reflexes. Seven or above is normal.
  • Weighed, measured, banded
  • Vitamin K injectionFor blood clotting. Strongly recommended.
  • Hearing screenQuick and painless.
  • They stay with usUnless there's a medical reason not to.

Going Home

How long we stay

  • Vaginal birthDischarged 24 to 48 hours after delivery.
  • C-sectionTwo to four days to recover.

Before they let us go

  • Bleeding controlled
  • You've passed urine
  • Baby feeding, and meconium passed

Then, at home

  • A public health nurse visitsStandard across most of Canada, and included. Weight, feeding, and how you are.
We cannot leave without a correctly fitted car seat. They check. Worth fitting it beforehand rather than in a hospital car park.

Part ThreeThe First Year

How many hours a day does a newborn sleep?

A) 8–10 hours
B) 12–14 hours
C) 14–17 hours
D) 20+ hours
In two to three hour chunksDay and night, no distinction. The longest stretch is three or four hours.
"Sleeping through" means 5–6 hoursNot the full night. The phrase has been oversold for decades.
And 2,500–3,000 nappiesIn year one. Eight to twelve a day at the start! We need to buy a job lot in advance.

The First Two Weeks

What we'll actually be doing

  • Feeding every 2–3 hoursAround the clock, nights included. This is the whole job.
  • Winding after every feedEvery time, whichever way we're feeding.
  • Keeping the cord stump dryIt falls off within one to three weeks. Sponge baths only until it does.

What we need to know

  • Standards drop everywhere except the babyThe flat can be a mess. Dinner can be pizza every day.
  • We say yes to helpAnyone who offers food or an hour of holding gets a yes.
  • Feeling shell-shocked is the normal outcomeNot a sign either of us is doing it wrong.

Feeding

Health Canada recommends breastfeeding for six months.

Breastfeeding

  • It often doesn't come naturallyThat's extremely common, and it isn't failure.
  • Hospital lactation consultants are freeUse them before we pay anyone. They're the best value in the whole system.
  • Cluster feeding is realHours of near-constant feeding, usually evenings. Exhausting, and it passes.
  • Get help early forNipple soreness, engorgement, or anything that looks like mastitis.

Formula and combination

  • Formula is nutritionally completeA fed baby is the main aim doesn't matter how we do it.
  • About $150–200 a monthSterilising properly matters more than the brand does.
  • Combination feeding works wellAnd it means I can help with night feeds, which you will defo need!

Sleep

The safety rules

  • On their back, every timeEvery sleep, day and night.
  • Firm flat mattress, nothing else in the cotNo pillows, bumpers or loose bedding.
  • Their own cot, in our room, for the first six months minimum
  • Not too warmComfortable for us is comfortable for them.
  • Never on a sofa or armchairApparently this catches a lot of parents out?

Getting through the nights

  • They have no body clock at firstNight means nothing to a newborn.
  • Bright and chatty by day, dark and dull by nightThat's how the clock gets built.
  • Swaddling and white noiseThe womb was loud and tight. Both help more than they sound like they would.
  • We tag teamProperly, in shifts, so one of us is always fully off duty.
  • Loose routine from 6–8 weeksBath, feed, sleep. Not a schedule.

Doctor, or Normal?

🔴 Go now

  • ANY fever under 3 months38°C or above is an emergency at that age. Not a judgement call.
  • Difficulty breathing
  • Seizures, floppy or unresponsive
  • A rash that doesn't fadePress a glass against it. If it stays, go.
  • DehydrationNo wet nappy for 6+ hours, sunken fontanelle, crying without tears.
  • Blood in stool or vomit

🟡 Within 24 hours

  • Persistent feverOver 3 months old.
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea
  • Not feeding well
  • A rash that does fade
  • Crying that sounds wrongWe'll know.

🟢 Entirely normal

  • Hiccups and sneezingConstantly.
  • Noisy, snuffly breathing
  • Blotchy skinVery common in the first weeks.
  • A pulsing fontanelleAlarming to look at, completely fine.
  • Crying for no reasonThe default state.
811 is HealthLink BC. Free, 24 hours, staffed by nurses. It exists precisely for the "is this normal?" call.

Looking After Us

Us

  • Most couples find year one the hardestKnowing that in advance is helpful!
  • Arguments about nothing are sleep deprivationNot us genuinely upset with each other
  • We don't keep scoreOn nappies, nights, or who is more tired.
  • Shifts, so someone is always properly offBroken sleep for two is worse than full sleep for one.
  • Five minutes of actually talking countsMost days that's all there'll be.

When it's more than that

  • Baby blues are normalDays three to ten, tearful and emotional, up to 80% of mothers. Hormonal, and it passes.
  • Postnatal depression is differentPersistent low mood, anxiety, feeling detached from the baby, lasting beyond two weeks.
  • More than 1 in 10 mothersAnd about 1 in 10 fathers too.
  • Common and treatableEarly help makes a large difference. It's a GP conversation, not a crisis.

Childcare in Vancouver

$10/day centres ~$200/mo Real, but genuinely scarce. Lovely if we get one, not something to plan around.

Waitlists run 12 to 18 months, so we register as soon as we're pregnant, not when we need it. The $900 reduction applies at about 94% of licensed centres, and the income-tested Affordable Child Care Benefit can cut it further, though probably not for us.

Maternity Leave & EI

Fifteen weeks, birth parent only, and it cannot be shared. Everything else is parental leave, which is the next slide.

Do we qualify?

  • 600 insurable hours eachWe both clear that comfortably.
  • EI replaces 55% of earningsUp to a cap, which is where it gets less generous than it sounds.

What it actually pays

  • $729 a week in 2026We'll both hit the cap immediately.
  • $3,159 a month, grossTaxable, and it's what Izzy would get.
  • Every dollar above $68,900 adds nothingWhich is why the drop feels much steeper than "55%".

Timing

  • Can start 12 weeks before the due dateMust end 17 weeks after birth.
  • Apply the moment you stop workingWaiting four weeks or more can lose benefits outright.
  • Apply for maternity and parental togetherOne application, not two.
Two things that catch people out. There's a one-week unpaid waiting period at the start of the claim, and EI under-withholds tax, so budget for a bill at filing rather than a refund. Also worth checking whether either employer tops up.

Parental Leave, Standard or Extended

This one is shareable. We both have to pick the same option, and there's no switching once payments start.

Standard

  • Up to 35 weeks eachMaximum 40 weeks shared, so my weeks come out of yours.
  • 55% of income, $729/week capThe same cap as maternity.
  • Plus 5 use-it-or-lose-it weeksOnly unlock if we both take leave, and mine are topped up to full base.both take leave.

Extended

  • Up to 61 weeks eachMaximum 69 weeks shared.
  • 33% of income, $437/week capLonger, but thinner.
  • Plus 8 use-it-or-lose-it weeksSame deal, bigger bonus.
The bit people get wrong. Eighteen months isn't less money overall, it's the same money spread thinner. One parent taking the lot gets about $36,450 over 12 months ($3,038/mo) on standard, or about $37,592 over 18 months ($2,088/mo) on extended. Extended is very slightly more in total. The real question is monthly cashflow, not the total.
And the bonus weeks are free money. Five extra weeks of payments that simply don't exist unless I take leave. I think I should take them.
