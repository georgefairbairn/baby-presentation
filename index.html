Part OnePregnancy

What is the first sense a baby develops in the womb?

A) Hearing
B) Sight
C) Touch
D) Taste
Touch, week 8Before most people have even had a scan. Sensitive across the whole body by week 16.
Hearing, week 22It's why people talk to the bump. They learn our voices before they ever see us.
Sight, lastNewborns focus at about 20–30cm. Roughly breast to face. Evolution is tidy like that.

Forty Weeks, Three Chapters

Each trimester has its own character. The first is hard and hidden, the second is the good one, the third is a waiting game.

First · Weeks 1–12

  • Every organ formsWhich is why this trimester matters most, and why it's the riskiest.
  • Dating scan at 8–10 weeksA blob with a heartbeat. Apparently surreal.
  • Morning sicknessAll day. The name is a lie. Usually eases by week 14.
  • Real exhaustionBlood volume climbs 50% while the body builds a placenta from nothing.

Second · Weeks 13–26

  • The golden oneNausea backs off, energy comes back. Most people feel best here.
  • First kicks, 18–22 weeksFlutters at first. Easy to mistake for indigestion.
  • Anatomy scan at 20 weeksThe big one. Also when we could find out the sex.
  • Stretching achesRound ligament pain, back pain, and the bump arrives.

Third · Weeks 27–40

  • Half a pound a week2lb at week 27, 6–9lb by birth.
  • Braxton HicksPractice contractions. Uncomfortable, not painful. Regular and painful is different.
  • Strep B swab at 35–37 weeksA quarter of women carry it. If positive, antibiotics in labour.
  • Sleep becomes impossibleNothing is comfortable. Nesting at 2am is hormonal, not a personality change.

What to Eat, What to Skip

The rules sound overwhelming written down. The short version is nothing raw, nothing mould-ripened, and more iron than feels necessary.

Plenty of

  • IronRed meat, spinach, lentils. Blood volume climbs hard, and low iron means even more exhaustion. Pair with vitamin C.
  • Oily fish, twice a weekBrain development. Capped at twice because of mercury.
  • Calcium and whole grainsDairy or fortified plant milk, leafy greens, legumes.
  • Folic acid, vitamin D, a prenatalFolic acid is the important one. It prevents neural tube defects, and we started in April.

None of

  • AlcoholThere's no known safe amount, so the answer is none.
  • Raw or undercooked anythingSushi, runny eggs, rare meat. Listeria and toxoplasmosis are rare but serious.
  • Soft cheese, pâté, deli meatsBrie, camembert, and yes, the prosciutto on a pizza.
  • High-mercury fishSwordfish and shark specifically.
Caffeine doesn't have to be zero. Health Canada says under 300mg a day, which is about two coffees.

Before We Start

Most of this is admin. Two bits have real deadlines, which is the only reason I'm putting it this early.

You

  • Folic acid, 400mcg dailyIdeally three months before we start.
  • Medication reviewSome prescriptions aren't safe in pregnancy.
  • Rubella and chickenpox immunityBoth are live vaccines, so they have to happen before conception with a month's gap after.
  • Cycle trackingSo we know the fertile window rather than guessing.

Me

  • Sperm takes ~3 months to turn overSo anything I change now shows up later, not immediately.
  • Less alcohol, more sleepUnglamorous, but it's the whole list.
  • Family historyWorth actually asking my parents rather than assuming.

If it takes a while

  • Roughly 1 in 4 per cycleAbout 85% of couples conceive within a year. A few months is the normal case, not the bad case.
  • Ask for help at 12 monthsSix if we're over 35. First tests are simple, and we both get tested.
  • BC funds one IVF cycleUp to $19,000 since July 2025. Income-tested, applicant must be 41 or under.
The plan is September. If it isn't September, that is still completely normal, and I don't want either of us treating month three as a failure.

Midwife or OB-GYN?

This isn't assigned like it would be on the NHS. We choose, and both are fully covered by MSP.

Midwife

  • The same person throughoutPregnancy, birth and after. You build an actual relationship.
  • No referral neededWe can approach a practice directly.
  • Longer appointments, home visits afterWhich sounds genuinely brilliant.
  • Hospital, birth centre or homeAll three are options.
  • Waitlists are realRegister the day we get a positive test.

OB-GYN

  • Specialist medical trainingThe right call for higher-risk pregnancies.
  • Usually needs a GP referralOne extra step to get started.
  • Can perform c-sectionsFull hospital team on hand.
  • Better for 35+, multiples, pre-existing conditionsNone of which currently applies to us.
  • May not be the one who deliversYou get whoever is on call that day.

🟡Weeks 28–36Every two weeks
🔴Week 36 onwardsEvery week until birth

When to Call

This is the slide I hope we never need. I've put it in traffic lights so it's memorable rather than frightening.

🔴 Call immediately

  • Heavy bleedingEspecially with cramping.
  • Severe abdominal pain
  • Baby stops movingThird trimester.
  • Preeclampsia signsSudden swelling, severe headache, vision changes.
  • High fever

🟡 Within 24 hours

  • Reduced movementReduced, not absent.
  • Painful urinationOften a UTI. Common in pregnancy.
  • Persistent vomiting
  • Unusual discharge

🟢 Next appointment

  • Mild spottingFirst trimester.
  • Braxton HicksPractice contractions.
  • General aches and swellingHeartburn too.
When in doubt, red. Nobody has ever been annoyed at a pregnant woman for calling. HealthLink BC is 811 if we just want to ask someone.

What Can Go Wrong

I nearly left this out. But I'd rather we'd both read it calmly now than be reading it on a phone at 3am.

Miscarriage

  • 1 in 8 known pregnanciesAround 80% of those happen in the first 12 weeks.
  • Chromosomal, about half the timeIt happens at conception. Not caused by anything either of us did.

Ectopic

  • 1 in 90 pregnanciesSharp one-sided pain, shoulder-tip pain, dizziness.
  • This one is A&ENot a phone call. Go.

Preeclampsia

  • After week 20Sudden swelling, severe headaches, vision changes.
  • More common in first pregnanciesWhich is us. Picked up at routine BP checks.

Gestational diabetes

  • Mid-second trimesterUsually no symptoms at all.
  • Routine screening catches itAnd it's manageable.
The reassuring half. Risk drops fast. 1.7% at 12 weeks, 0.5% by 16, and it falls significantly once there's a heartbeat. Around 85% of pregnancies go on healthily. Even after two or three losses, 75% of couples still get there. What we can control, we control. The rest genuinely isn't on us.

Our Birth Plan

Less a plan than a one-page note telling the room what we'd prefer if nobody has time to ask.

What goes on it

  • WhereHospital, birth centre or home.
  • Who's in the roomUs, a doula, family, or nobody.
  • Pain reliefWhat you'd like to try first, and how you feel about intervention.
  • Skin-to-skin and cord clampingDelayed clamping if all is well.
  • FeedingBreast, bottle or both.

How to treat it

  • Draft it end of the second trimesterDiscuss it with our provider around week 36.
  • One page, maximumNobody reads two pages during a labour.
  • Preferences, not promisesMost birth plans change on the day, and that isn't a failure.
The real point of writing one is that it forces the conversation now, when we're calm, instead of at 4cm.

Part TwoBirth & Hospital

How long is the average first-time labour?

A) 4–6 hours
B) 8–10 hours
C) 12–18 hours
D) 24+ hours
Only 4–8 of those are activeThe rest is early labour, and most of it is best spent at home.
Second babies are much fasterOften half the time. Nobody's first is the benchmark.
It's an average, not a targetShorter and longer are both completely normal.

How Labour Actually Goes

Four stages. The first is by far the longest, and most of it happens before we go anywhere.

1a · Early labour

  • 0 to 6cmThe longest stage by a distance.
  • Contractions start irregularSlowly building to a rhythm.
  • Best spent at homeEat, bath, rest, distract. Sleep if it starts at night.

1b · Active labour

  • 6 to 10cmThis is the serious part.
  • Stronger, longer, closer togetherThe pattern tightens up.
  • Pain relief decisions land hereUsually not before.

2 · Pushing

  • 20 minutes to 3 hoursFor a first baby. Huge range.
  • CrowningThe "ring of fire". Intense stretch, then numbness.
  • Head, shoulder, then all at onceAnd then straight onto your chest.

3 · The placenta

  • Nobody warns you about this oneThere is a second delivery.
  • 5 to 30 minutesWith an injection. Up to an hour without.
  • Much easierThan what just happened.
5-1-1 is when we go. Contractions five minutes apart, lasting one minute, for one hour. Also go for waters breaking, heavy bleeding, or reduced movement. And go anyway if your instinct says go.

Pain Relief Options in Canada

All available, all covered. Worth having a first preference so nobody has to ask you a question mid-contraction.

EpiduralThe most effective by far. Needs an anaesthetist, available at most hospitals.
Nitrous oxideGas and air. Widely available, take it or leave it between contractions.
IV opioidsMorphine or fentanyl. Takes the edge off rather than removing it.
WaterBath or shower. Genuinely good in early labour.
TENS machineBest used early. Needs hiring in advance, so it's a thing to remember.
Nothing at allBreathing, movement, massage, hypnobirthing. A real option, not a lesser one.
You can change your mind at any point. The only catch is that not every option is available at every stage, so earlier decisions have more choices in them.

My Job on the Day

I wanted to write this down so it's a commitment rather than a good intention.

Before and on the way

  • Bag packed by week 36Not week 40. Packed, in the hall, done.
  • Route known, car seat fittedAnd the number saved in both our phones.
  • Early labour is mineKeep you comfortable, time the contractions, stay calm about it.

At the hospital

  • I know the birth planSo you never have to explain it while in labour.
  • I'm your advocateI ask the questions and I push back if something feels wrong.
  • Present. No phone.Hand rub, back rub, cold flannel, water. That's the whole job.
  • AfterSkin-to-skin if you can't, cut the cord, take the photos, tell everyone later.

The First Few Hours

A lot happens quickly, most of it routine. Knowing the order means none of it is alarming.

The golden hour

  • Skin-to-skin, straight awayRegulates their temperature, heart rate and breathing. Starts the bonding.
  • Delayed cord clampingWaiting 1–3 minutes measurably boosts their iron stores.
  • First feed within the hourIf it works. If it doesn't, that's also fine.
  • If you can't, I do itMy chest works nearly as well.

You

  • Stitches if neededUnder local anaesthetic.
  • Vitals watched for a couple of hoursStandard, not a warning sign.
  • Take the pain reliefIbuprofen and paracetamol together work properly. Don't be brave about it.
  • You might be shakingThat's adrenaline, and it's normal.
  • Bleeding lasts 4–6 weeksMaternity pads, not tampons.

Baby

  • Apgar at 1 and 5 minutesHeart rate, breathing, colour, tone, reflexes. Seven or above is normal.
  • Weighed, measured, banded
  • Vitamin K injectionFor blood clotting. Strongly recommended.
  • Hearing screenQuick and painless.
  • They stay with usUnless there's a medical reason not to.
Protect this bit. Visitors can wait. Nobody needs to meet them in the first two hours except us.

Going Home

How long we stay

  • Vaginal birthDischarged 24 to 48 hours after delivery.
  • C-sectionTwo to four days to recover.

Before they let us go

  • Bleeding controlled
  • You've passed urine
  • Baby feeding, and meconium passed

Then, at home

  • A public health nurse visitsStandard across most of Canada, and included. Weight, feeding, and how you are.
We cannot leave without a correctly fitted car seat. They check. Worth fitting it and having it inspected weeks beforehand rather than in a hospital car park.

Part ThreeThe First Year

How many hours a day does a newborn sleep?

A) 8–10 hours
B) 12–14 hours
C) 14–17 hours
D) 20+ hours
In two to three hour chunksDay and night, no distinction. The longest stretch is three or four hours.
"Sleeping through" means 5–6 hoursNot the full night. The phrase has been oversold for decades.
And 2,500–3,000 nappiesIn year one. Eight to twelve a day at the start. Buy in advance.

The First Two Weeks

Everyone I've asked describes this fortnight the same way, which is that it's a blur and nobody feels competent.

What we'll actually be doing

  • Feeding every 2–3 hoursAround the clock, nights included. This is the whole job.
  • Winding after every feedEvery time, whichever way we're feeding.
  • Keeping the cord stump dryIt falls off within one to three weeks. Sponge baths only until it does.

What we should agree now

  • Standards drop everywhere except the babyThe flat can be a mess. Dinner can be toast.
  • We say yes to helpAnyone who offers food or an hour of holding gets a yes.
  • Feeling shell-shocked is the normal outcomeNot a sign either of us is doing it wrong.

Feeding

Health Canada recommends breastfeeding for six months. It's also the single most common thing for people to feel awful about, so I want us clear on it in advance.

Breastfeeding

  • It often doesn't come naturallyThat's extremely common, and it isn't failure.
  • Hospital lactation consultants are freeUse them before we pay anyone. They're the best value in the whole system.
  • Cluster feeding is realHours of near-constant feeding, usually evenings. Exhausting, and it passes.
  • Get help early forNipple soreness, engorgement, or anything that looks like mastitis.

Formula and combination

  • Formula is nutritionally completeA fed baby is the requirement. Everything past that is preference.
  • About $150–200 a monthSterilising properly matters more than the brand does.
  • Combination feeding works wellAnd it means I can take a night feed, which matters for both of us.
My position, in advance. if breastfeeding is making you miserable, we switch, and I won't need convincing.

Sleep

The safety rules, which aren't negotiable

  • On their back, every timeEvery sleep, day and night.
  • Firm flat mattress, nothing else in the cotNo pillows, bumpers or loose bedding.
  • Their own cot, in our room, six months minimum
  • Room at 16–20°CCooler than feels right.
  • Never on a sofa or armchairThis is the one that catches exhausted parents out.

Getting through the nights

  • They have no body clock at firstNight means nothing to a newborn.
  • Bright and chatty by day, dark and dull by nightThat's how the clock gets built.
  • Swaddling and white noiseThe womb was loud and tight. Both help more than they sound like they would.
  • We tag teamProperly, in shifts, so one of us is always fully off duty.
  • Loose routine from 6–8 weeksBath, feed, sleep. Not a schedule, an order.
🌙3–4 monthsLonger stretches start
😴6–12 monthsMost sleep through
😰4, 8 and 12 monthsRegressions, just as we think we've cracked it

0–2 months

Doctor, or Normal?

Same traffic lights. The top-left box is the one worth memorising properly.

🔴 Go now

  • ANY fever under 3 months38°C or above is an emergency at that age. Not a judgement call.
  • Difficulty breathing
  • Seizures, floppy or unresponsive
  • A rash that doesn't fadePress a glass against it. If it stays, go.
  • DehydrationNo wet nappy for 6+ hours, sunken fontanelle, crying without tears.
  • Blood in stool or vomit

🟡 Within 24 hours

  • Persistent feverOver 3 months old.
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea
  • Not feeding well
  • A rash that does fade
  • Crying that sounds wrongWe'll know.

🟢 Entirely normal

  • Hiccups and sneezingConstantly.
  • Noisy, snuffly breathing
  • Blotchy skinVery common in the first weeks.
  • A pulsing fontanelleAlarming to look at, completely fine.
  • Crying for no reasonThe default state.
811 is HealthLink BC. Free, 24 hours, staffed by nurses. It exists precisely for the "is this normal?" call, and we should use it without feeling silly.

Looking After Us

This is the slide I most wanted to put in, and the one with the least information on it.

Us

  • Most couples find year one the hardestKnowing that in advance is genuinely most of the help.
  • Arguments about nothing are sleep deprivationNot us going wrong. Worth naming it out loud when it happens.
  • We don't keep scoreOn nappies, nights, or who is more tired.
  • Shifts, so someone is always properly offBroken sleep for two is worse than full sleep for one.
  • Five minutes of actually talking countsMost days that's all there'll be.

When it's more than that

  • Baby blues are normalDays three to ten, tearful and emotional, up to 80% of mothers. Hormonal, and it passes.
  • Postnatal depression is differentPersistent low mood, anxiety, feeling detached from the baby, lasting beyond two weeks.
  • More than 1 in 10 mothersAnd about 1 in 10 fathers, which nobody ever asks about.
  • So I'm saying it plainlyIt applies to me too, and I'd rather you watched for it in me than assumed I was fine.
  • Common and treatableEarly help makes a large difference. It's a GP conversation, not a crisis.

What does the average Canadian family spend in the first year?

A) $5,000–$8,000
B) $8,000–$12,000
C) $12,000–$25,000
D) $30,000+
Childcare is the whole spreadIt's the difference between the bottom and the top of that range.

But the spend isn't the problemThe income drop is. That's three slides away.

What It Costs

One-time setup

  • Car seat, $150 to $500Buy new. Second-hand only if we know its full history, because an accident writes one off invisibly.
  • Cot and mattress, $200 to $800New mattress, cot can be used.
  • Monitor, $50 to $300
  • Second-hand is fine forPram, changing table, bouncer, and essentially all of it.

Every month

  • Nappies, $80 to $120
  • Wipes and toiletries, $30 to $50
  • Formula, if we use it, $150 to $200
  • Baby food from 6 months, $50 to $100
  • Clothes, $50 to $100Buy second-hand. They're in each size for about two months.

The ones people forget

  • Pelvic floor physio, about $120 a sessionNot covered by MSP. Budget for it rather than treating it as optional.
  • Doula, if we want one, $1,200 to $2,500One-off, not covered.
  • Lactation consultant, $150 to $250Privately. Hospital ones are free.
  • Classes from about 3 months, $50 to $150Plus a buffer of $100–200 for the thing we didn't think of.
Diarise this one. Most Canadian employer benefit plans give us only 30 days from the birth to add the baby. Miss it and we wait until the next open enrolment.

Childcare in Vancouver

The scary one, but less scary than the sticker price suggests.

$10/day centres ~$200/mo Real, but genuinely scarce. Lovely if we get one, not something to plan around.
Typical licensed centre ~$1,100/mo Advertised at $2,000, minus the $900 CCFRI reduction that comes off automatically.
Home-based or nanny $1,200–$2,500+/mo Depends on hours and setup. Nanny shares sit at the bottom of this range.
Waitlists run 12 to 18 months, so we register as soon as we're pregnant, not when we need it. The $900 reduction applies at about 94% of licensed centres, and the income-tested Affordable Child Care Benefit can cut it further, though probably not for us.

Maternity Leave & EI

Fifteen weeks, birth parent only, and it cannot be shared. Everything else is parental leave, which is the next slide.

Do we qualify?

  • 600 insurable hours eachWe both clear that comfortably.
  • EI replaces 55% of earningsUp to a cap, which is where it gets less generous than it sounds.

What it actually pays

  • $729 a week in 2026We'll both hit the cap immediately.
  • $3,159 a month, grossAnd it's taxable.
  • Every dollar above $68,900 adds nothingWhich is why the drop feels much steeper than "55%".

Timing

  • Can start 12 weeks before the due dateMust end 17 weeks after birth.
  • Apply the moment you stop workingWaiting four weeks or more can lose benefits outright.
  • Apply for maternity and parental togetherOne application, not two.
Two things that catch people out. There's a one-week unpaid waiting period at the start of the claim, and EI under-withholds tax, so budget for a bill at filing rather than a refund. Also worth checking whether either employer tops up.

Parental Leave, Standard or Extended

This one is shareable. We both have to pick the same option, and there's no switching once payments start.

Standard

  • Up to 35 weeks eachMaximum 40 weeks shared.
  • 55% of income, $729/week capThe same cap as maternity.
  • Plus 5 use-it-or-lose-it weeksThey only unlock if we both take leave.

Extended

  • Up to 61 weeks eachMaximum 69 weeks shared.
  • 33% of income, $437/week capLonger, but thinner.
  • Plus 8 use-it-or-lose-it weeksSame deal, bigger bonus.
The bit people get wrong. Eighteen months isn't less money overall, it's the same money spread thinner. One parent taking the lot gets about $36,450 over 12 months ($3,038/mo) on standard, or about $37,592 over 18 months ($2,088/mo) on extended. Extended is very slightly more in total. The real question is monthly cashflow, not the total.
And the bonus weeks are free money. Five extra weeks of payments that simply don't exist unless I take leave. I think I should take them.

What This Actually Costs Us

Our real numbers, not averages. Household gross is currently about $32,300 a month.

Both working $32,300 Per month, gross. Where we are today.
You on leave $21,000 Down $11,300 a month, or 35%. My salary plus your $729 a week.
Both on leave $6,300 Down $26,000 a month, or 80%. Fine for a fortnight. Not for months.
Over the whole first year, with you taking 50 weeks and me taking 5, household income drops from about $388,000 to about $234,000. That's roughly $154,000 less, a 40% cut.
Three things that soften or sharpen it. Your taxable income falls from $173,500 to about $36,500, so your average tax rate drops a long way and the net hit is gentler than the gross. But we earn too much for the Canada Child Benefit, which phases out around $220k, so budget for $0. And the RESP grant isn't income-tested. $2,500 a year in gets $500 free regardless.

The House & The Baby

You said this might not be relevant. I think it's the most relevant slide in the deck, because the two plans compete for the same money and the same months.

Timing the mortgage

  • Lenders discount EI incomeMany won't count it in full, so we should close while we're both on full salary.
  • The deposit and the leave buffer are one potWe cannot count the same money twice. That's the actual tension.
  • No nursery rushBaby is in our room for six months anyway, so we don't need the extra bedroom on day one.

If we move areas

  • Childcare lists don't travelThey're 12–18 months and neighbourhood-specific. Moving can cost us our place.
  • Provider and hospital follow postcodeA move mid-pregnancy can mean changing midwife and where you deliver.
  • Don't move at 36 weeksOr with a two-week-old. The second trimester is the window, when you'll have the most energy.
The cleanest order I can see. Conceive, get pre-approved and close while we're both earning, move in the second trimester, then go on leave. February onwards works fine as long as the mortgage lands before the leave does.
