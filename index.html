What to Eat, What to Skip

Plenty of

  • IronRed meat, spinach, lentils. Pair with vitamin C.
  • Oily fish, twice a weekBrain development. Capped because of mercury.
  • Calcium and whole grainsDairy or fortified plant milk, leafy greens, legumes.
  • Folic acid, vitamin D, a pregnancy multivitaminPrevents neural tube defects. Started in April.

None of

  • AlcoholNo known safe amount.
  • Raw or undercooked anythingSushi, runny eggs, rare meat. Listeria and toxoplasmosis.
  • Soft cheese, pâté, deli meatsBrie, camembert, prosciutto on pizza.
  • High-mercury fishSwordfish and shark.
Caffeine doesn't have to be zero. Health Canada says under 300mg a day. About two coffees.

Before We Start

You

  • Folic acid, 400mcg dailyIdeally three months before we start.
  • Medication reviewSome prescriptions aren't safe in pregnancy.
  • Rubella and chickenpox immunityLive vaccines. Must happen before conception, with a month's gap after.
  • Cycle trackingTo find the fertile window.

Me

  • Sperm takes ~3 months to turn overChanges now show up in three months.
  • Less alcohol, more sleep
  • Family historyWorth asking both sets of parents.

If it takes a while

  • Roughly 1 in 4 per cycleAbout 85% of couples conceive within a year.
  • Ask for help at 12 monthsSix months if over 35. Both of us get tested.
  • BC funds one IVF cycleUp to $19,000 since July 2025. Income-tested, applicant must be 41 or under.
The plan is September. If it isn't September, that's still completely normal.

When to Call

🔴 Call immediately

  • Heavy bleedingEspecially with cramping.
  • Severe abdominal pain
  • Baby stops movingThird trimester.
  • Preeclampsia signsSudden swelling, severe headache, vision changes.
  • High fever

🟡 Within 24 hours

  • Reduced movementLess than usual, not absent.
  • Painful urinationOften a UTI.
  • Persistent vomiting
  • Unusual discharge

🟢 Next appointment

  • Mild spottingFirst trimester.
  • Braxton HicksPractice contractions.
  • General aches and swellingHeartburn too.
When in doubt, red. HealthLink BC is 811, free and 24 hours.

What Can Go Wrong

Miscarriage

  • 1 in 8 known pregnanciesAround 80% of those happen in the first 12 weeks.
  • Chromosomal, about half the timeHappens at conception. Not caused by anything we did.

Ectopic

  • 1 in 90 pregnanciesSharp one-sided pain, shoulder-tip pain, dizziness.
  • This one is A&ENot a phone call.

Preeclampsia

  • After week 20Sudden swelling, severe headaches, vision changes.
  • More common in first pregnanciesPicked up at routine BP checks.

Gestational diabetes

  • Mid-second trimesterUsually no symptoms at all.
  • Routine screening catches itManageable once found.
Risk drops fast. 1.7% at 12 weeks, 0.5% by 16, lower again once there's a heartbeat. About 85% of pregnancies go on healthily, and 75% even after two or three losses.

Part TwoBirth & Hospital

How long is the average first-time labour?

A) 4–6 hours
B) 8–10 hours
C) 12–18 hours
D) 24+ hours
Only 4–8 of those are activeThe rest is early labour, mostly at home.
Second babies are much fasterOften half the time.
It's an average, not a targetShorter and longer are both completely normal.

How Labour Actually Goes

1a · Early labour

  • 0 to 6cmThe longest stage.
  • Contractions start irregularSlowly building to a rhythm.
  • Best spent at homeEat, bath, rest, distract. Sleep if it starts at night.

1b · Active labour

  • 6 to 10cm
  • Stronger, longer, closer together
  • Pain relief decisions land hereUsually not before.

2 · Pushing

  • 20 minutes to 3 hoursFor a first baby.
  • CrowningThe "ring of fire". Intense stretch, then numbness.
  • Head, shoulder, then all at onceThen straight onto your chest.

3 · The placenta

  • Nobody warns you about this oneThere is a second delivery.
  • 5 to 30 minutesWith an injection. Up to an hour without.
  • Much easierThan what just happened.
5-1-1 is when we go. Five minutes apart, one minute long, for one hour. Also for waters breaking, heavy bleeding or reduced movement.

Pain Relief Options in Canada

All available in BC, all covered.

EpiduralMost effective. Needs an anaesthetist.
Nitrous oxideGas and air. Take it or leave it between contractions.
IV opioidsMorphine or fentanyl. Takes the edge off.
WaterBath or shower. Good in early labour.
TENS machineBest used early. Hire in advance.
Nothing at allBreathing, movement, massage, hypnobirthing.
You can change your mind at any point. Not every option is available at every stage.

The First Few Hours

The golden hour

  • Skin-to-skin, straight awayRegulates their temperature, heart rate and breathing. Starts the bonding.
  • Delayed cord clampingWaiting 1–3 minutes boosts their iron stores.
  • First feed within the hourIf it works.
  • If you can't, I do it

You

  • Stitches if neededUnder local anaesthetic.
  • Vitals watched for a couple of hoursStandard.
  • Take the pain reliefIbuprofen and paracetamol together.
  • You might be shakingAdrenaline. Normal.
  • Bleeding lasts 4–6 weeksMaternity pads.

Baby

  • Apgar at 1 and 5 minutesHeart rate, breathing, colour, tone, reflexes. Seven or above is normal.
  • Weighed, measured, banded
  • Vitamin K injectionFor blood clotting. Strongly recommended.
  • Hearing screenQuick and painless.
  • They stay with usUnless there's a medical reason not to.
Visitors can wait. The first couple of hours are ours.

Going Home

How long we stay

  • Vaginal birthDischarged 24 to 48 hours after delivery.
  • C-sectionTwo to four days to recover.

Before they let us go

  • Bleeding controlled
  • You've passed urine
  • Baby feeding, and meconium passed

Then, at home

  • A public health nurse visitsStandard across most of Canada. Weight, feeding, and how you are.
We can't leave without a correctly fitted car seat. Fit it and get it checked weeks beforehand.

Part ThreeThe First Year

How many hours a day does a newborn sleep?

A) 8–10 hours
B) 12–14 hours
C) 14–17 hours
D) 20+ hours
In two to three hour chunksDay and night, no distinction. The longest stretch is three or four hours.
"Sleeping through" means 5–6 hoursNot the full night.
And 2,500–3,000 nappiesIn year one. Eight to twelve a day at the start. Buy in advance.

The First Two Weeks

What we'll actually be doing

  • Feeding every 2–3 hoursAround the clock, nights included.
  • Winding after every feedEvery time, whichever way we're feeding.
  • Keeping the cord stump dryIt falls off within one to three weeks. Sponge baths only until it does.

What we should agree now

  • Standards drop everywhere except the babyThe flat can be a mess. Dinner can be toast.
  • We say yes to helpAnyone who offers food or an hour of holding gets a yes.
  • Feeling shell-shocked is normalNot a sign either of us is doing it wrong.

Feeding

Health Canada recommends six months.

Breastfeeding

  • It often doesn't come naturallyCommon, and not a failure.
  • Hospital lactation consultants are freeUse them before we pay anyone.
  • Cluster feeding is realHours of near-constant feeding, usually evenings.
  • Get help early forNipple soreness, engorgement, or anything that looks like mastitis.

Formula and combination

  • Formula is nutritionally complete
  • About $150–200 a monthSterilising properly matters more than the brand does.
  • Combination feeding works wellMeans I can take a night feed.
If breastfeeding is making you miserable, we switch. I won't need convincing.

Sleep

The safety rules, which aren't negotiable

  • On their back, every timeEvery sleep, day and night.
  • Firm flat mattress, nothing else in the cotNo pillows, bumpers or loose bedding.
  • Their own cot, in our room, six months minimum
  • Room at 16–20°C
  • Never on a sofa or armchairThis is the one that catches exhausted parents out.

Getting through the nights

  • They have no body clock at first
  • Bright and chatty by day, dark and dull by night
  • Swaddling and white noiseThe womb was loud and tight.
  • We tag teamIn shifts, so one of us is always fully off.
  • Loose routine from 6–8 weeksBath, feed, sleep.
🌙3–4 monthsLonger stretches start
😴6–12 monthsMost sleep through
😰4, 8 and 12 monthsRegressions, just as we think we've cracked it

Doctor, or Normal?

🔴 Go now

  • ANY fever under 3 months38°C or above. No judgement call at that age.
  • Difficulty breathing
  • Seizures, floppy or unresponsive
  • A rash that doesn't fadePress a glass against it. If it stays, go.
  • DehydrationNo wet nappy for 6+ hours, sunken fontanelle, crying without tears.
  • Blood in stool or vomit

🟡 Within 24 hours

  • Persistent feverOver 3 months old.
  • Vomiting or diarrhoea
  • Not feeding well
  • A rash that does fade
  • Crying that sounds wrong

🟢 Entirely normal

  • Hiccups and sneezing
  • Noisy, snuffly breathing
  • Blotchy skinVery common in the first weeks.
  • A pulsing fontanelleAlarming to look at. Fine.
  • Crying for no reason
811 is HealthLink BC. Free, 24 hours, staffed by nurses. Exactly for the "is this normal?" call.

Looking After Us

Us

  • Most couples find year one the hardest
  • Arguments about nothing are sleep deprivationWorth naming out loud when it happens.
  • We don't keep scoreOn nappies, nights, or who is more tired.
  • Shifts, so someone is always properly offBroken sleep for two is worse than full sleep for one.
  • Five minutes of actually talking countsMost days that's all there'll be.

When it's more than that

  • Baby blues are normalDays three to ten, tearful and emotional, up to 80% of mothers. Hormonal, and it passes.
  • Postnatal depression is differentPersistent low mood, anxiety, feeling detached from the baby, lasting beyond two weeks.
  • More than 1 in 10 mothersAnd about 1 in 10 fathers.
  • It applies to me tooWatch for it in me rather than assuming I'm fine.
  • Common and treatableEarly help makes a big difference. It's a GP conversation.

Childcare in Vancouver

$10/day centres ~$200/mo Real, but scarce. Not something to plan around.
Typical licensed centre ~$1,100/mo Advertised at $2,000, minus the $900 CCFRI reduction that comes off automatically.

Waiting lists run 12 to 18 months. Register as soon as we're pregnant. The $900 reduction applies at about 94% of licensed centres.

Maternity Leave & EI

Fifteen weeks, birth parent only. Cannot be shared.

Do we qualify?

  • 600 insurable hours eachWe both clear that comfortably.
  • EI replaces 55% of earningsUp to a cap.

What it actually pays

  • $729 a week in 2026We'll both hit the cap immediately.
  • $3,159 a month, grossAnd it's taxable.
  • Every dollar above $68,900 adds nothingWhich is why the drop feels much steeper than "55%".

Timing

  • Can start 12 weeks before the due dateMust end 17 weeks after birth.
  • Apply the moment you stop workingWaiting four weeks or more can lose benefits.
  • Apply for maternity and parental togetherOne application, not two.
Two catches. A one-week unpaid waiting period, and EI under-withholds tax, so budget for a bill at filing. Worth checking whether either employer tops up.

Parental Leave, Standard or Extended

Shareable. Same option for both of us, no switching later.

Standard

  • Up to 35 weeks eachMaximum 40 weeks shared.
  • 55% of income, $729/week capThe same cap as maternity.
  • Plus 5 use-it-or-lose-it weeksThey only unlock if we both take leave.

Extended

  • Up to 61 weeks eachMaximum 69 weeks shared.
  • 33% of income, $437/week capLonger, but thinner.
  • Plus 8 use-it-or-lose-it weeksSame rule, bigger bonus.
Eighteen months isn't less money. It's the same money spread thinner. About $36,450 over 12 months ($3,038/mo) on standard, or $37,592 over 18 months ($2,088/mo) on extended. The question is monthly cashflow.
The bonus weeks are free money. Five extra weeks that don't exist unless I take leave.

What This Actually Costs Us

Household gross is about $32,300 a month.

Both working $32,300 Per month, gross. Where we are today.
You on leave $21,000 Down $11,300 a month, or 35%. My salary plus your $729 a week.
Both on leave $6,300 Down $26,000 a month, or 80%. Fine for a fortnight. Not for months.
Over the whole first year, with you taking 50 weeks and me taking 5, household income drops from about $388,000 to about $234,000. That's roughly $154,000 less, a 40% cut.
Worth knowing. Your taxable income falls from $173,500 to about $36,500, so the net hit is gentler than the gross. We earn too much for the Canada Child Benefit, which phases out around $220k, so budget $0. The RESP grant isn't income-tested. $2,500 a year in gets $500 free.

The House & The Baby

Timing the mortgage

  • Lenders discount EI incomeMany won't count it in full, so we should close while we're both on full salary.
  • The deposit and the leave buffer are one potWe cannot count the same money twice.
  • No nursery rushBaby is in our room for six months anyway, so we don't need the extra bedroom on day one.

If we move areas

  • Childcare lists don't travelThey're 12–18 months and neighbourhood-specific. Moving can cost us our place.
  • Provider and hospital follow postcodeA move mid-pregnancy can mean changing midwife and where you deliver.
  • Don't move at 36 weeksOr with a two-week-old. The second trimester is the window.
The cleanest order. Conceive, close the mortgage while we're both earning, move in the second trimester, then go on leave. February onwards works as long as the mortgage lands before the leave does.
